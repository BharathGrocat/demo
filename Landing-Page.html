Five shifts shaping how India's families steward wealth

Across every theme, one pattern repeats: structure is replacing instinct, and the next generation is stepping forward rather than drifting away.

78%

From preservation to purpose

Rank protecting family wealth, values and continuity as a top priority. Almost half now also want to grow beyond the core business.

56%

Growth, on the family's terms

Put long-term capital appreciation and compounding first. Calculated risk wins over high-stakes bets, and global investing is now mainstream.

3 models

Power is being redistributed

Founders favour family-led with advisors, the second generation professional management, and the third a hybrid of both.

54%

The succession cycle

Of third-generation families say their next generation already influences or co-leads major decisions.

24%

The family office evolves

Set one up to shift from running the operating business to a diversified portfolio. It is maturing into an institution of governance.

Families hold the reins tightest during the second handover, then loosen their grip by the third.

The assumption that each generation drifts further from the business does not hold. Involvement dips in the second generation as families lean on structure, then rises sharply in the third.

Nearly half of third-generation leaders expect to be actively running and scaling the business within four to five years, driven less by obligation than by the opportunity to take it into its next phase of growth.
