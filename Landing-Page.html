Next generation of Indian wealth

India is entering a decisive decade of intergenerational transition. We spoke with family business leaders and wealth custodians across generations, representing wealth ranging from approximately INR 100 crore to INR 10,000 crore, to understand how they invest, decide, prepare their successors and build the family offices that will carry their legacy forward.

Key takeaways from the study

India’s next generation of wealth custodians are not simply inheriting wealth; they are inheriting responsibility. They are taking a broader view of family wealth, treating succession as an ongoing process and bringing a more shared approach to decision-making.

  1. 68%

    A broader view of family wealth.

    Say today’s custodians are more likely than previous generations to step back, reflect and view family wealth as a whole.

  2. 54%

    Succession as ongoing planning.

    Say succession is increasingly being treated as ongoing planning rather than inheritance by default.

  3. 41%

    Greater comfort going global.

    Point to greater comfort with global exposure and cross-border decisions.

  4. 42%

    Alignment often comes before documentation.

    Of families have broad alignment on succession that is not yet formalised, while 36% have a clear, documented succession plan.

  5. Shared

    Big decisions are increasingly made together.

    The most common model is a joint process involving family members and professional management, even as the head of the family retains the final call in some cases. The shift is about creating greater clarity, accountability and institutional strength around family decision-making, not moving it away from the family.

  6. 3 models

    Each generation wants a different governance model.

    Founders favour family-led structures supported by advisers; second-generation leaders prefer professional management with family oversight; third-generation respondents increasingly favour hybrid models.

The true measure of intergenerational wealth is not merely how well it is preserved, but whether each generation has the confidence and freedom to redefine its purpose.

Role of the family office is to help transform inheritance into responsible stewardship, protecting the family’s legacy while enabling new enterprise, impact and relevance.

Sreepriya NSCo-Founder & CEO, Entrust Family Office

Families hold the reins tightest during the second handover, then loosen their grip by the third.

Among first-generation families, 36% said the next generation already has meaningful influence over decisions or co-leads in specific areas of the business. This falls to 18% in the second generation, before rising sharply to 54% in the third.

The assumption that each generation drifts further from the business does not hold. Involvement dips in the second generation as families lean on structure and governance, then rises sharply in the third, driven less by obligation than by the opportunity to take the business into its next phase of growth.
