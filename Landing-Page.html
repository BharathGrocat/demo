Next generation of Indian wealth

India is entering a decisive decade of intergenerational transition. We spoke with family business leaders and wealth custodians across three generations, representing wealth from INR 100 crore to INR 10,000 crore, to understand how they invest, decide, prepare their successors and build the family offices that will carry their legacy forward.

Eight takeaways from the study

The next generation is not simply inheriting wealth; it is inheriting responsibility. Here is what that looks like in practice.

  1. 78%

    Preservation is the shared instinct, but no longer the whole story.

    Rank protecting family wealth, values and continuity among their top priorities, and 46% also want to grow and diversify beyond the core business.

  2. 68%

    Today’s custodians see the whole picture.

    Say their generation’s defining difference is the ability to step back and view family wealth as a whole portfolio, not just the operating business.

  3. 56%

    Growth, on the family’s own terms.

    Put long-term capital appreciation and compounding first. Families favour calculated risk, and global investing is now mainstream, led by public equities, private markets and listed funds (44%).

  4. 46%

    The third generation would rather build than allocate.

    Of third-generation leaders, given a billion dollars and no rules, would buy businesses or controlling stakes first, against 8% of founders.

  5. 3 models

    Power is being redistributed, not given away.

    Founders favour a family-led model with advisors; the second generation prefers professional management with oversight; the third settles on a hybrid.

  6. 54%

    Succession is a cycle, not a slow drift.

    Of third-generation families say the next generation already influences or co-leads decisions, up from 18% in the second. Nearly half expect to be running the business within four to five years.

  7. 30%

    Direction exists, documentation lags.

    Have committed succession to a documented plan, although about two-thirds of families have a sense of direction.

  8. 24%

    The family office is becoming an institution of governance.

    Set one up to shift from running the business to managing a diversified portfolio. Starting over, families would first invest in technology and reporting systems.

“

Good governance is not tested when everyone agrees. It is tested when they don’t.

The families who navigate this well have answered the hard questions before disagreement arrives: who owns what, who runs what, and how differences get resolved. That is the real purpose of structure: not to replace judgement, but to ensure no family is inventing the rules in the middle of a crisis.

Sreepriya NSCo-Founder & CEO, Entrust Family Office

Families hold the reins tightest during the second handover, then loosen their grip by the third.

The assumption that each generation drifts further from the business does not hold. Involvement dips in the second generation as families lean on structure and governance, then rises sharply in the third, driven less by obligation than by the opportunity to take the business into its next phase of growth.
